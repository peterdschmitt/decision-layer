# Daily post process

Peter's ask (2026-10-11): a blog post every day, emailed to him, with a way for him and Claude to edit it together. Posts are built around use cases that show the problem and the result, and the most important part is explaining how Jev works, including how it was trained. This file is the routine's checklist. The editorial rules in [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md) still apply.

## Each day

1. **Pick the news.** One fresh story from an approved outlet (HANDOFF.md, "Headline outlets"), not one used as a lead in the last 7 days (see the daily post log in project memory). Verify the quote word for word at the source. If no approved outlet has a fresh story you can verify, ask Peter instead of stretching the list.
2. **Pick the use case.** Favor insurance claims, underwriting and agency operations. One concrete, made-up example (synthetic data only).
3. **Write it like a sharp human expert, not a report** (Peter, 2026-10-29, on the first fence draft: "this is total slop!"). The bar is a blog post a busy agency owner would forward, not a machine write-up.
   - **The top of the page sells the post** (Peter, 2026-10-31: "especially title, byline and attention grabbing information. The blog can be longer. It could contain a summary or snapshot."). Write several versions of each and keep the best. In order, the page shows:
     - **Title** (`title`): the H1. Short, concrete and surprising, under about 12 words. Lead with the moment or the number, not the product. Good: “The scanner said 2028. The invoice said 2026. Jev caught it.” Bad: “Five reasons Jev is the right checker for claim paperwork”.
     - **Dek** (`dek`): two or three sentences that say the problem, the result and what the reader gets from the post.
     - **Byline** (`author`, `audience`): who wrote it and who it's for, e.g. “For agency owners and service teams who handle claim paperwork”. The page adds dates and the model version.
     - **Stat strip** (`stats`, `statsNote`): two to four big numbers, each one stated and sourced in the body, with a note saying where they come from and what they are not (e.g. “Not our test”). Leave it out rather than stretch.
     - **Snapshot** (`snapshot`): five labeled lines: The problem, What Jev does, What the test showed (or What the docs say), What it means for you, What we haven't done. It replaces the old “In short” box; keep `summary` too, as the email's plain-text fallback.
     - **Why this matters today**: the news hook from `respondingTo` (lede, quote, impact), shown as a box under the snapshot.
   - **8 to 10 minutes is fine.** After the reasons and the proof, add “What your team would see”, “How to try it in two weeks” (steps on work they've already done) and “Questions your team will ask” (three or four plain Q&As, including cost and “could it miss something?”).
   - **Always a news hook.** Lead (the `respondingTo` block) with a fresh story from an approved outlet and a verbatim, verified quote, then what it means for the reader. The story doesn't have to be about Jev: AI, insurance, claims, storms and regulation all work if the post earns the link. Verify the quote at the outlet or its primary source (court filing, SEC filing, official post). "No news today" is a last resort; say so in the thread first.
   - **Why Jev is better comes first** (Peter, 2026-10-29: "we need to clearly explain why Jev is better ... it takes too long to get to the reasons"). Right under the news hook, a "Why Jev is better for this job" section: three to five numbered, bold reasons, each comparing Jev with what the reader would otherwise use (a person searching, a chatbot, a rule, a classifier) and each backed by TypeSafe's docs or independent reporting. Then the use case, as the proof.
   - **Then a scene.** A real-feeling moment at an agency or claims desk (made up, and plain that it's made up where it matters), not a definition.
   - **One clear point.** Say what Jev should and shouldn't do in this situation, and why it matters to the agency. Cut everything that doesn't serve it.
   - **Short sections** with human headings. Plain words; explain any technical term once, in passing.
   - **No boilerplate.** No "Nth in our series" list, no "note on today's news", no repeated summary. The snapshot must not repeat the dek word for word.
   - **Hedge once.** One "We haven't run this yet" line in "The fine print", not "expected, not measured" all through the post.
   - **Show it at the desk.** A short table of who does what (service person, Jev, carrier), then the messier case where it should say "I don't know".
   - **Code last.** One short "For your developer" section at the end, or a link. No code in the middle of the story.
   - **Sources stay honest.** Every claim about Jev links to TypeSafe's docs or independent reporting. Never claim tests or reviews that didn't happen.
4. **Check for overlap with earlier posts.** Read every earlier post and draft (published and unpublished). Each post owns one "how Jev works" topic; see the series map below, pick a topic not yet covered, and add it to the map. Don't re-explain what an earlier post covers: link to it in one line instead. Run the quote check (no verbatim quote, longer than a few words, should appear in two posts) and fix any repeats. Open the new post with a one-line note saying where it sits in the series.
5. **Edit for a human voice** (several passes: cut filler, no em-dashes, no hype words, short sentences).
6. **Create the shared draft** as a Claude Doc titled "Decision Layer draft, <date>: <title>", with the full post text, the preview link and a short "what to check" list. Peter edits there or comments; Claude folds his edits back into the post on the next pass.
7. **Push** the post to a branch `claude/post-<date>` cut from the newest unpublished draft branch (so every draft and its cross-links show in one preview), or from `main` once the drafts are merged. Vercel builds a preview.
8. **Email Peter the whole post** at peter@converselyai.com, subject "Decision Layer draft for <date>: <title>". The email IS the blog post, formatted like the site (navy and orange header, title, dek, byline, stat strip, snapshot, why-this-matters box, every section, tables, code, In the news, sources), not a summary (Peter, 2026-10-28: "this current version is so cryptic its useless", "i need way more detail"). Render it with `node scripts/post-email.mjs <post.md> <preview share URL of the post> <doc URL> > email.html`, write a short plain-text fallback (title, the dek, both links), then send with `node scripts/send-draft-email.mjs "<subject>" body.txt email.html` (prefix `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1 NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS=/root/.ccr/ca-bundle.crt` in cloud sessions). Needs `RESEND_API_KEY` and `RESEND_FROM` in the cloud environment, with network access set to Full. Get the share URL from the Vercel tool `get_access_to_vercel_url`. Screenshot the HTML at phone width before sending.
   - **Plain language and real detail.** The "In short" lines and the post must make sense to a busy agency owner on first read: no tech jargon like "regex", "extraction", "generative model", "threshold" or "API" without a plain explanation. Every post includes a "Why it matters to an agency" section and a step-by-step walk-through of the example (a table: step, who does it, what happens), plus what happens on a messier case.
9. **Reply in the project thread** with the same two links.
10. **Publish only when Peter says "publish"** (merge to `main`).

## How Peter's edits come back

- Edits or comments in the Claude Doc: on the next run (or when Peter says "pick up my edits"), Claude reads the doc, applies the changes to the post file, rebuilds the preview and says what changed.
- Replies to the email or messages in the thread work the same way.
