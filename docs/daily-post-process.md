# Daily post process

Peter's ask (2026-10-11): a blog post every day, emailed to him, with a way for him and Claude to edit it together. Posts are built around use cases that show the problem and the result, and the most important part is explaining how Jev works, including how it was trained. This file is the routine's checklist. The editorial rules in [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md) still apply.

## Each day

1. **Pick the news.** One fresh story from an approved outlet (HANDOFF.md, "Headline outlets"), not one used as a lead in the last 7 days (see the daily post log in project memory). Verify the quote word for word at the source. If no approved outlet has a fresh story you can verify, ask Peter instead of stretching the list.
2. **Pick the use case.** Favor insurance claims, underwriting and agency operations. One concrete, made-up example (synthetic data only).
3. **Write for leaders, about the pairing** (Peter, 2026-10-31: "I think you are assuming this site is just for insurance people or low level employees", "It's using decision models along with llms to improve the quality and robustness of highly actionable outcomes to minimize slop, improve outcome legitimacy and improve speed to accurate action. For fractions of the cost and dramatic improvements in speed."). Earlier rules still hold: no report-style "slop" (Oct 29), reasons early (Oct 29), a strong top (Oct 31).
   - **Macro, not micro** (Peter, 2026-10-31: "These are way to micro. It's not about did it catch a simple line in an email. It about how to improve x percent"). Every post is about moving a business number by a percentage: cost per case, cycle time, touchless or straight-through rate, error rate, revenue, retention. Lead with a sourced benchmark gap (e.g. average vs best-in-class), explain what drives it, then show how the LLM + decision model pairing moves that driver. A single caught error is evidence for the mechanism, one short section, never the headline.
   - **Written for a demanding executive** (Peter, 2026-10-31: "written for an executive that demands detail. Why does this matter? Why should it make a difference? Why spend time understanding. Most importantly. How will this make me money, improve my customer relationships, generate more revenue"). Every post answers, with sourced numbers: why it matters, what it's worth in money, what it does for customers, and what it does for revenue. Where the revenue case is indirect, say so plainly rather than stretch. Multiply sourced figures out for one realistic volume and label it as arithmetic, not a forecast. Name the cost the numbers leave out (building the workflow).
   - **Loop every draft through lenses before sending** (Peter, 2026-10-31: "Are you looping through this with different lenses", "What about editors and publishers"). One pass each, fixing what each finds: a skeptical CFO (is every number sourced, what's the payback, what's left out?), a CRO or head of revenue (where's the revenue?), a COO (how would we run it, who owns it?), a head of customer experience (what do customers feel?), a skeptic (any claim we can't back?), a line and copy editor (clarity, rhythm, cut fluff), and a publisher or editor-in-chief (would the headline, angle and lede earn a top business publication's front page?). Tell Peter which lenses changed what.
   - **Audience:** executives, operations leaders and builders deciding how AI runs their processes. Insurance is one worked example, never the audience. Show how the idea carries to other functions (payables, onboarding, logistics, billing, support).
   - **Thesis, every post:** an LLM and a decision model working in tandem. The LLM reads, drafts and reasons; the decision model answers narrow questions about what the LLM produced, with a probability, at full volume; rules you own decide what moves now and what goes to a person. The payoff: less slop, more legitimate (auditable) outcomes, faster to the right action, a fraction of the cost. Every example pairs the two and ends in an action. No examples that use a decision model alone.
   - **The analogy is the tractor and the hydraulics** (LLM = tractor, decision model = hydraulics): a force multiplier, immense power, lifting loads with little effort. Never frame the decision model as brakes, a checker, an editor or a source of precision; Peter rejected those.
   - **The category, not just Jev.** Jev is the best-documented model, but name rivals where they fit (OpenAI Decisions API, Microsoft, Cloudflare, AWS and others; see project memory "decision-model-category"). Only state what a source supports; say plainly what we haven't checked.
   - **The top of the page sells the post** (Peter, 2026-10-31: "especially title, byline and attention grabbing information. The blog can be longer. It could contain a summary or snapshot."). Write several versions of each and keep the best. In order, the page shows:
     - **Title** (`title`): short, concrete and surprising, under about 12 words, leading with the moment or the number. Good: “The AI read 15 numbers. A decision model flagged the 3 that were wrong.” Bad: “Five reasons Jev is the right checker for claim paperwork”.
     - **Dek** (`dek`): two or three sentences: the problem leaders have, what the pairing does about it, what the reader gets from the post.
     - **Byline** (`author`, `audience`): e.g. “For leaders deciding how AI runs their operations”. The page adds dates and the model version.
     - **Stat strip** (`stats`, `statsNote`): two to four big numbers, each stated and sourced in the body, with a note saying where they come from and what they are not (e.g. “Not our test”). Leave it out rather than stretch.
     - **Snapshot** (`snapshot`): five labeled lines: Why it matters, Where the money is, Why AI alone hasn't delivered it, What to do, What we haven't done. Keep `summary` as three plain lines for the email fallback.
     - **Why this matters today**: the news hook from `respondingTo` (lede, quote, impact). The impact paragraph says what the story teaches leaders about running AI in a process.
   - **Always a news hook.** A fresh story from an approved outlet and a verbatim, verified quote. It doesn't have to be about Jev: any decision model, AI in business, regulation or a relevant industry story works if the post earns the link. Verify the quote at the outlet or its primary source (court filing, SEC filing, official post). "No news today" is a last resort; say so in the thread first.
   - **Body, in order (10 to 12 minutes):**
     1. The gap: the sourced benchmark numbers and what drives them.
     2. "Why AI alone hasn't closed it".
     3. "The tractor and the hydraulics": the analogy applied to today's driver.
     4. "The math for a <volume> operation": a table of the sourced figures multiplied out, the decision model's cost, and the build cost the numbers leave out.
     5. "What it's worth: money, customers and revenue": one sourced paragraph each.
     6. The evidence for the mechanism (a published test or the vendor's docs), short.
     7. "Levers leaders control": baseline, question design, the dial by risk, shadow mode, an owner, the paper trail.
     8. "The same math in other processes": one line each.
     9. "How to find your X%": a formula they can run on their own numbers.
     10. "Which decision model for this job": Jev and rivals that fit, sourced, and what we haven't checked.
     11. "Questions leaders will ask", "The fine print" (hedge once), and a short "For your developer" code section last.
   - **Plain words, human voice.** Short sections, human headings, any technical term explained once in passing. No "Nth in our series" list, no "note on today's news". The snapshot must not repeat the dek word for word.
   - **Sources stay honest.** Every claim about a model links to the vendor's docs or independent reporting. Label vendor benchmark numbers as vendor claims. Never claim tests or reviews that didn't happen.
4. **Check for overlap with earlier posts.** Read every earlier post and draft (published and unpublished). Each post owns one pairing pattern or "how decision models work" topic; see the series map below, pick a topic not yet covered, and add it to the map. Don't re-explain what an earlier post covers: link to it in one line instead. Run the quote check (no verbatim quote, longer than a few words, should appear in two posts) and fix any repeats. Open the new post with a one-line note saying where it sits in the series.
5. **Edit for a human voice** (several passes: cut filler, no em-dashes, no hype words, short sentences).
6. **Create the shared draft** as a Claude Doc titled "Decision Layer draft, <date>: <title>", with the full post text, the preview link and a short "what to check" list. Peter edits there or comments; Claude folds his edits back into the post on the next pass.
7. **Push** the post to a branch `claude/post-<date>` cut from the newest unpublished draft branch (so every draft and its cross-links show in one preview), or from `main` once the drafts are merged. Vercel builds a preview.
8. **Email Peter the whole post** at peter@converselyai.com, subject "Decision Layer draft for <date>: <title>". The email IS the blog post, formatted like the site (navy and orange header, title, dek, byline, stat strip, snapshot, why-this-matters box, every section, tables, code, In the news, sources), not a summary (Peter, 2026-10-28: "this current version is so cryptic its useless", "i need way more detail"). Render it with `node scripts/post-email.mjs <post.md> <preview share URL of the post> <doc URL> > email.html`, write a short plain-text fallback (title, the dek, both links), then send with `node scripts/send-draft-email.mjs "<subject>" body.txt email.html` (prefix `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1 NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS=/root/.ccr/ca-bundle.crt` in cloud sessions). Needs `RESEND_API_KEY` and `RESEND_FROM` in the cloud environment, with network access set to Full. Get the share URL from the Vercel tool `get_access_to_vercel_url`. Screenshot the HTML at phone width before sending.
   - **Plain language and real detail.** The "In short" lines and the post must make sense to a busy agency owner on first read: no tech jargon like "regex", "extraction", "generative model", "threshold" or "API" without a plain explanation. Every post includes a "Why it matters to an agency" section and a step-by-step walk-through of the example (a table: step, who does it, what happens), plus what happens on a messier case.
9. **Reply in the project thread** with the same two links.
10. **Publish only when Peter says "publish"** (merge to `main`).

## How Peter's edits come back

- Edits or comments in the Claude Doc: on the next run (or when Peter says "pick up my edits"), Claude reads the doc, applies the changes to the post file, rebuilds the preview and says what changed.
- Replies to the email or messages in the thread work the same way.
