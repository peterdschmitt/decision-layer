# Use-case catalog

A running list of every real use case of a decision model coupled with an LLM (or used in a process an LLM feeds) that research turns up. Every daily run adds what its full research finds. Posts draw from here; the post queue is built from here (Peter, 2026-10-31 14:46Z, "Change all five").

**Labels:** **published case** (a named company's deployment, self-reported by the company or vendor), **vendor claim** (a vendor's own demo or docs, no named customer), **community demo** (a builder's post or repo, self-reported), **independent result** (third-party or peer-reviewed). **Unverified** means we saw it only second-hand. Keep thin items in with their label; drop only what is fabricated or contradicted.

Columns: what the LLM does, what the decision model does, the numbers as published, source, label, date checked.

**Independent evidence on predicting ad performance:**

- LLM-only judging is weak. In LOLA (Upworthy A/B tests), pure LLMs were barely above random; arXiv 2609.25010 found Kendall τ = 0.361.
- Training on outcomes helps. Meta's AdLlama gave +6.7% CTR, but this is company research.
- Kantar LINK AI matches its surveys about 7 in 10 times. That is a vendor claim.
- Nielsen attributes about 47% of sales lift to creative.

**Defensible use:** high-volume labelling and compliance flags, joined to the advertiser's own results or used as the prior for A/B tests, not as a replacement for testing.

## A. Named companies: production, or company tests on their own data

- **Microsoft XBOX Research (gaming, player research)**. LLM: none in the loop; GPT-6 Sol is the comparator. Microsoft-Decision-1: sorts "more than 10,000 open-ended pieces of feedback and reviews from surveys, STEAM, and Twitter/X... into a fixed set of themes established by researchers". Numbers: "competitive on quality with GPT-6 Sol while running over 14 times faster and 200 times less expensive." Source: https://commandline.microsoft.com/microsoft-decision-1-model-foundry/ (Achint Srivastava, 2026-10-09). **Published case (vendor's own team, internal test).**

- **Microsoft on-call engineering (incident response)**. LLM: "Our on-call engineers use AI to retrieve relevant knowledge to respond to live incidents." Decision-1: does the knowledge retrieval step. Numbers: "performed better and faster than an LLM for knowledge retrieval" (no figures given). Source: same post, 2026-10-09. **Published case (vendor internal, no numbers).**
- **Microsoft Discovery (scientific R&D agents)**. LLM: the agent "evaluates a previous experiment, revises its approach". Decision-1: gives the rubric grades that drive replanning. Numbers: "46 times more consistent than the LLM-based score at three times the speed and resulted in nearly four times the speed on adaptive replanning". Source: same post, 2026-10-09. **Published case (vendor internal).**
- **Browserbase / Stagehand (browser automation)**. LLM: the fallback for any action below a 0.7 acceptance threshold. Jev: answers "which candidate is best" and "does any candidate match" for each `act()` call. Numbers: "In early testing, Act median latency drops from 1.97 seconds to 0.46 seconds which is about 4.3× faster (or 77% less time)." Source: https://www.browserbase.com/blog/what-is-jev (2026-09-21); repeated in LangChain's https://www.langchain.com/blog/building-prod-with-jev-and-langgraph (2026-09-25). **Published case (company's own framework, early testing).**
- **Browser Use (browser agents)**. LLM: "small LLM fallback to type". Jev: picks the element to act on at each step ("new action space every step"). Numbers: "Findings flights took 7s and cost only $0.0039". The repo browser-use/jev-ultrafast had about 23k stars on 2026-10-15 (madewithjev count). Source: https://github.com/browser-use/jev-ultrafast, Gregor Zunic's X post (via https://madewithjev.com/builds/browser-use-flights) and HN item 49735979 (2026-09-17). **Community demo (company-built, open source).**
- **MotherDuck (cloud data warehouse, SQL analytics)**. LLM: the older path classified rows with an LLM. Jev: `prompt_jev()`, a SQL function that returns a label, score or yes/no for each row. Numbers: "Text classification in MotherDuck just got about 50x faster at about 1% of the cost... On a 100,000-row benchmark it matched frontier LLM accuracy in 40 seconds for fifty cents. The comparable LLM took more than half an hour and cost $37." Their table: Jev 89%, $0.50, 40 s; gpt-5.6-terra 88%, $37.58, 31m59s; gpt-4o-mini 80%, $1.93. The benchmark is AG News (a public dataset), not customer data. Shipped "on all paid MotherDuck plans". Source: https://motherduck.com/blog/motherduck-supports-jev/ (2026-09-21). **Published case (shipped product feature; vendor benchmark on public data).**
- **Rox (AI for sales teams)**. LLM: the Rox agent answers the user's query. Jev Score: grades retrieved chunks from "transcripts, emails, CRM notes, news, and documents" on a four-level scale, so they can be reranked. Numbers: "We used Jev to retrieve sales data 20x faster, 10x cheaper, and 12% more accurate than GPT-5 Mini." Method: "75 production-realistic queries against a benchmark set by GPT-6 Astra on high reasoning." Source: https://x.com/rox_ai/status/2104642687534293353 and the method post /2104642689455292721 (2026-09-28). **Published case (offline test, company post).**
- **Glean (enterprise search)**. LLM: the existing baseline for routing and for citation judging (GPT-5.6 Luna). Jev: picks an expert route, reranks up to 50 results, and judges citation coverage. Numbers: on routing, 66.8% agreement and a "median per-entry speedup of 8.1×"; on reranking, "$0.00044 per query and 0.195 s at p50 offline", but production-order tie-breaks lifted Recall@6 from 40.2% to 44.3%, which the author flags; on citations, more repeatable than Luna even at xhigh reasoning. "We plan to launch Jev in some of these use cases" once data residency and other operational readiness are in place. Source: Tony Gentilcore's X article "Is Jev overhyped? We tested it on 4 real enterprise tasks" (https://x.com/tonygentilcore/status/2104639390266036251, 2026-09-28). The body was read in an Arabic repost (https://youmind.com/ar/landing/x-viral-articles/jev-enterprise-benchmark-test). **Published case (offline eval; original article body not directly opened).**

It returns "the probability and confidence of each agent being the relevant one"; "If the confidence is below 0.4, it will return a list of the three most likely agents for the user to pick", and it answers yes or no on whether an agent should reply in a watched channel. Numbers: none on performance; 66 routable agents in a 300-person company; "we could adopt it in our router two weeks into the project." Source: https://www.sanity.io/engineering/how-we-built-our-company-agent-harness (2026-10-22).
